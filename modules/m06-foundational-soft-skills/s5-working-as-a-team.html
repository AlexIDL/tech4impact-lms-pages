Section 5: Working as a Team
Time: 15 minutes
Track: HEI & VET

By the end of this section, you will be able to:

  • Describe what effective collaboration looks like in a small team
  • Explain the role of clear communication in preventing and resolving team conflict
  • Define negotiation and apply a basic negotiation approach to a team disagreement
The team hits a wall

SharedTable is growing. Four months in, the app is running, ten shops are participating, and the community kitchen is receiving regular donations. Yet inside the team, something is wrong.

Ana wants to expand to a second city. Jonas thinks they should fix what isn’t working before they consider growing. Mira has stopped speaking up in meetings.

The team is not failing because of a technical problem. They are failing because they have not learned how to work together.

Collaboration

Collaboration means working with others towards a shared goal — not just dividing tasks or reporting back, but actually building something together.

In a social venture, collaboration is not optional. No one person has all the skills, networks or perspectives needed to succeed. Ana is strong at communication. Jonas is methodical and careful. Mira is the most creative. SharedTable reflects all three of them, but only because they found a way to work together despite their differences.

Effective collaboration requires a few things that don’t come automatically:

  • Shared understanding of the goal. If team members have different ideas of what success looks like, they will pull in different directions without realising it. Ana sees success as scale. Jonas sees it as reliability. Until they name that difference, they cannot resolve it.
  • Clear roles. People collaborate better when they know what they are responsible for and what falls to others. Undefined overlap and ambiguity breed resentment.
  • Psychological safety. Mira stopped speaking up because her last two ideas were not really considered by her teammates. When people don’t feel safe to contribute, the team loses access to their thinking. Effective collaboration requires an environment where everyone’s input is genuinely welcomed.

Communication

Communication is how the team stays aligned. It is not just presenting information — it is ensuring the other person has understood you, and that you have understood them.

Most team communication problems are not about what was said. They are about what was assumed. When Jonas told Ana “I think we should wait,” he assumed she understood his concern was about quality. Ana heard hesitation and a lack of ambition. Neither of them checked.

Two practices make a significant difference:

Say the reason, not just the position

“I think we should wait” is a position. “I think we should wait because two shops have already flagged issues I want us to fix before we grow further” is a reason. The reason is what the other person needs to engage with your thinking.

Check understanding before moving on

Especially in a disagreement — take a breath, separate emotion from logic, then paraphrase what you heard and confirm your understanding is correct. This slows the conversation just enough to prevent much longer problems later.

Negotiation

Negotiation is the process of reaching an agreement when people want different things — finding an outcome both people can commit to. It is not about winning.

The most common mistake is arguing over positions rather than exploring the interests behind them.

Ana’s position: expand now. Jonas’s position: wait. These seem incompatible. But their underlying interests are not:

  • Ana wants SharedTable to prove it can work outside their city before the academic year ends.
  • Jonas wants to protect the venture’s reputation from a rushed rollout.

When they talk about interests rather than positions, a third option becomes visible: expand to one new city, but only after the two outstanding issues are fixed — which Jonas estimates will take two weeks. Neither got exactly what they wanted. Both can commit to the outcome. That is a successful negotiation.

A simple negotiation approach

  1. State your position clearly.
  2. Explain the interest behind it — what you are actually trying to protect or achieve.
  3. Ask the other person to do the same.
  4. Look for an option that addresses as many shared interests as possible.

Multiple choice

1. Ana and Jonas are arguing about whether to expand. Ana says “expand now”; Jonas says “wait.” What should they do to negotiate effectively?

  • Put the decision to a team vote
  • Explore the interests and concerns behind each position
  • Ask a mentor outside the team to decide
  • Postpone the decision until everyone agrees

Answer: b)

2. Mira stopped contributing ideas in team meetings. According to this section, what is the most likely reason?

  • She ran out of ideas
  • She disagreed with the team’s goal
  • She didn’t feel safe to contribute
  • She lacked the communication skills to express herself

Answer: c)

3. Jonas tells Ana “I think we should wait” without explaining why. Which communication practice is he failing to use?

  • Checking understanding before moving on
  • Saying the reason, not just the position
  • Active listening
  • Suspending assumptions

Answer: b)

4. Which of the following is NOT one of the three conditions for effective collaboration listed in this section?

  • Shared understanding of the goal
  • Psychological safety
  • A designated team leader
  • Clear roles
