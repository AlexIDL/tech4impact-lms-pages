Section 6: Leading a Team
Time: 15 minutes
Track: HEI & VET

By the end of this section, you will be able to:

  • Define leadership as a behaviour rather than a role or personality type
  • Distinguish between leading as a founder and leading as a team member
  • Identify at least two behaviours that characterise effective leadership in a social venture context
Who is leading?

Six months into SharedTable, the team has grown. Ana, Jonas and Mira have been joined by Kwame, who manages shop partnerships, and Lena, who handles communications. The app is definitely working. The team is not.

Decision-making has slowed down. Kwame has strong views about approaching new shops but does not share them because he does not think his views will make a difference. Lena sends updates nobody reads because they are too long and too frequent. Everyone expects Ana — the original founder — to make every final decision. Ana does not want this responsibility either.

The team has a leadership problem that cannot be fixed by simply putting one person in charge.

What leadership actually is

Leadership is not a title. It is not a fixed personality type. It is not something you either have or don’t.

Leadership is a behaviour: helping a group move toward a goal. This definition matters because it means anyone on a team can lead — and in a well-functioning team, everyone does, at different moments and in different ways.

Having a founder, a president, or a project manager does not guarantee leadership. Titles create authority. Leadership is earned through action.

In a social venture this is especially true. Problems are complex, resources are limited, and people often care more about the mission than about hierarchy. Leadership that relies on authority alone tends to fail. Leadership that earns trust tends to succeed.

Leading as a founder

When you start a social venture, leadership comes with the territory. You set the direction, make early decisions, and carry the most risk. People look to you.

But founder leadership carries a specific danger: it can become a bottleneck. Ana is talented and committed. She is also the reason decisions are slow — because the team has stopped developing its own judgment. When Ana is unavailable, nothing moves.

Effective founder leadership is not about staying at the centre. It is about building a team that can function without you making every decision. That means:

Founder

Delegate with trust

Not just handing off tasks, but giving people genuine ownership of an area — and letting them make decisions within it, even ones you might not have made yourself.

Founder

Make your reasoning visible

When you explain why you made a decision, the team learns to make similar decisions independently. When you only announce outcomes, the team stays dependent on you.

Founder

Create space for others to lead

Actively step back in areas where someone else has more expertise, better relationships, or a stronger read on the situation.

Leading as a team member

You do not need to be the founder to lead. Some of the most important leadership in a social venture comes from people with no formal authority at all.

Kwame has strong views about how to approach new shops. He has built relationships with six of them already — all of whom eventually agreed to donate food. He knows what works and what doesn’t, but often he finds himself waiting for permission to share that knowledge or to put his ideas into action.

Team member

Contribute without waiting to be asked

If you know something that would help the group, say it. Leadership is not only the responsibility of the person at the top.

Team member

Surface what the group is not saying

Every team has things it avoids. The person who names them — calmly and constructively — performs one of the most valuable leadership acts there is.

Team member

Commit once a decision is made

Once a decision has been made and you have had the chance to input, commit to it fully. Teams that relitigate every decision become frozen.

Team member

Take responsibility for your area

You do not need to be in charge of everything. Take genuine ownership of what is yours and you are already leading.

The leadership moment

At the next team meeting, Ana tries something different. Instead of opening with the agenda, she asks Kwame to lead the first part of the session — a review of how to build shop partnerships.

Kwame is ready. He walks the team through what he has learned, what is working and what is not, and what he recommends. Ana asks questions rather than giving answers. Two decisions that had been stalled for weeks get made in twenty minutes.

After the meeting, Lena asks if she can take ownership of the communications calendar. In the following week, Jonas volunteers to document key decision-making processes so the whole team can access them.

Nobody changed their title. The team changed how it worked.

Leadership in a social venture is not concentrated in one person. It is distributed across the team — expressed differently depending on the role, the moment, and the person. A founder who leads well creates more leaders. A team member who leads well makes the whole team stronger. The goal is not to be the person everyone depends on, but to be part of a team that does not need to depend on any one person.

  • Contribute his expertise without waiting to be asked
  • Raise the issue only if Ana invites him to

Answer: c)
